# Prior art: who has done something like this

Searched 23 Oct 2026. This goes with [research.md](research.md).

How to read this:
- Facts come with links.
- Lines marked **(our analysis)** are my reading of what a result means for our pod.
- I read abstracts, press and summaries, not full papers. Before you quote a number in the deck, open the paper and check it.

---

## 1. The short answer

**Yes, lots of people have built seed balls, seed pellets and drone seed pods.** It's an active research field, with new papers in 2026. But **nobody has published a drone-dropped pod for these six UAE species, designed so seeds of different sizes each sit at the right depth.** That gap is where you can be original.

What the evidence says overall:
1. **Pods usually help seeds *emerge*, but often don't help plants *survive* long-term.** This shows up again and again (Johnstone 2026, Stock 2020, NZ 2025, a global meta-analysis in 2024). The Nabat field test counts germination over 8 to 10 weeks, which is the stage where pods help most.
2. **Results in the lab often don't carry over to the field.** A meta-analysis says so directly. Test outdoors if you can, even on a balcony.
3. **The recipe details matter, and studies disagree.** 60% clay was best in Brazil, but 10% clay beat 55% clay in Montana. Biochar and water crystals made balls too fragile in New Zealand. You have to test your own mix.
4. **Water is the bottleneck in the desert.** A small rain can start seeds germinating without keeping the seedlings alive.
5. **The leading UAE expert on these exact plants is at the University of Sharjah:** Prof. Ali El-Keblawy's group. They've written reviews on seed coating, scarification and priming for dryland restoration.

## 3. Everything found, by type

### 3.1 Large aerial seeding programmes that use pellets

**China (Mu Us sandy land, Alxa, Taklimakan)**
- **Su et al. 2017** (*J Zhejiang Univ Sci B*) tested **superabsorbent polymer (SAP) coatings** for aerial seeding in desert. On *Caragana korshinskii* in the Mu Us, the best coating **doubled the number of seedlings** compared with bare seed. *Haloxylon* species were "very sensitive" to SAP concentration (0.05% noted). Coated *Calligonum alaschanicum* seeds with polyacrylamide *or starch-type* SAP had "proper disintegration time and strength". It also found late May to mid-June was the best seeding time *there*. ([Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/api/getPdf?pmcid=PMC5565517), [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1631/jzus.B1600350))
  - **(our analysis)** Polyacrylamide is off-limits here (no microplastics), but the **starch-type** version is fair game. This paper is your best citation for a coated *Calligonum* seed being dropped from the air. Read it in full.
- **Alxa League pelleting (news feature):** pellets weigh at least twice the seed, and the coating is designed to **crack so the seed germinates only once there's enough rain**. Before pelleting, many seeds missed their target areas, and germination and survival were low. ([CN Focus](http://www.cnfocus.com/innovation-empowers-the-battle-against-desertification-and/))
  - **(our analysis)** A coating that waits for rain is exactly concept E in research.md (a pod that breaks down on cue). This is precedent for it.
- **Pelleting vs no pelleting, *Arid Zone Research* (Chinese):** 4 pelleted and 4 non-pelleted sites. Every measure improved more on pelleted sites. Read it cautiously. ([abstract](https://www.geores.com.cn/zgsm/CN/abstract/article/1000-694X/67167))
- **Taklimakan "seed ball" aerial trial over 50,000 mu (about 3,300 ha):** lighter seed balls gave more coverage from the plane, and the manager said "even a 1% survival rate is a considerable achievement". ([Sinoway Carbon](https://en.sinowaycarbon.com/i/5wanmushuz))
  - **(our analysis)** That's how hard real desert is. In the deck, compare your results with bare seed, not with 100%.
- **A 2004 Chinese study:** a multifunctional seed-dressing agent raised seedling survival by 9 to 12% in aerial seeding.
- **Long-term Mu Us results:** aerial seeding restored ecosystem health, plant diversity and soil microbes. ([Sustainability 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/su142215241), [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10634302/))

**India (Thar Desert, Rajasthan)**
- Seed pellets of *Cenchrus ciliaris*, *Acacia tortilis* and *Colophospermum mopane* were sown on 56 ha in Barmer district, by **helicopter and by hand**. **Throwing pellets by hand did better than the helicopter.** ([1999 report, timarit.is](https://timarit.is/page/7316218?lang=en))
  - **(our analysis)** How a pod lands matters. Pods from the air may bounce, roll or land in bad spots. That supports a flat, non-rolling shape.

**Kenya**
- **Seedballs Kenya:** seeds in **charcoal dust** with binders, to protect against birds, rodents and insects. Dropped from aircraft, including by the Kenya Defence Forces, who dropped 500,000 acacia seedballs in Isiolo. Wikipedia notes there's no published data showing aerial seed balls work. ([Seedballs Kenya](https://www.globallandscapesforum.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Seedballs-Kenya-Overview-GLF-2018-compressed.pdf), [Kenya MoD](https://www.mod.go.ke/?p=37602), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_seeding))

**Sahel / Great Green Wall**
- Seed balls of clay and compost. In Niger (a Hohenheim University project), wood ash and urine were used as local fertiliser in the balls. Senegal reported over 95% emergence (I couldn't check the study behind that). FAO trained people in drone seeding in Mauritania in 2024. The Great Green Wall overall has had **seedlings die off** from low rain and grazing. ([Borgen Project](https://borgenproject.org/?p=554337), [Feed the Future](https://www.feedthefuture.gov/?p=8089), [Yale e360](https://e360.yale.edu/digest/great-green-wall-stalls))

**Saudi Arabia**
- Drones are seeding royal reserves (for example, 300,000 sidr seeds). The National Center for Vegetation Cover has collected over 80 tonnes of native seed and is seeding 33 sites. A Saudi vendor says it drops "biodegradable seed pods". ([Middle East AI News](https://www.middleeastainews.com/p/can-drones-reforest-the-gulf-region), [Impact One](https://impact.one/company-news/seeds-of-renewal-desert-restoration-in-saudi-arabia/), [Terra Drone Arabia](https://terra-drone.com.sa/drone-applications-for-environmental-from-desertification-to-disaster-response/))
- **KAUST** (Himanshu Mishra) works on soil amendments (CarboSoil, SandX), not pods. In a two-year trial with 580 acacias, treated plots stored carbon. ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2647454/saudi-arabia))
- **KFUPM student project**, "Reducing Desertification by Drones' Seeds Dispersal": they dispersed seeds by drone and said the design needs work for wind and sandstorms. ([KFUPM](https://teamdesign.kfupm.edu.sa/projects/1828/))

**Kuwait**
- KISR restored the *Rhanterium epapposum* community and runs mass production of native seeds and plants. ([KISR symposium](https://www.kisr.edu.kw/media/filer_public/c2/ff/c2ff706e-2860-469c-a2cf-291ec6af6289/international_symposium.pdf))

### 3.2 Controlled studies of seed balls and pellets

| Study | Where | Key finding | What it means for us (our analysis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Johnstone et al. 2026**, *Restoration Ecology* | Arid US | Balls helped **emergence but not establishment**. **No difference by shape, clay amount, nutrients or microbes.** No long-term harm or benefit | Don't over-invest in fancy additives. Emergence is where pods help |
| **Teichroew & Rew 2025**, *Restoration Ecology* e14349 (doi:10.1111/rec.14349; online Nov 2024) | Semi-arid Montana | Greenhouse: seedlings came from 40% of pellets, and **2.5 cm pellets** were more likely to produce a seedling. Field: **10% clay pellets** gave the most seedlings (2.6 by season end, vs 1.2 for 55% clay and 1.0 for controls). How fast the pellet broke down didn't matter | **Less clay may be better in the field.** Supports our 2.5 cm size. Test low and high clay |
| **Stock et al. 2020**, *Frontiers Ecol Evol* | Arid Pilbara | A 1:1 topsoil and sandy waste pellet was best. **100% topsoil set hard and did worse.** Organic amendment **reduced** grass emergence. Only the grass survived; the shrubs emerged then died | Pods can set hard and trap seeds. Organic matter isn't always good. Shrubs are harder than grasses |
| **Organic-enriched seed balls 2026**, *Plant and Soil* | Arid land | 10% organic gave 48 to 92% germination, vs under 10% for direct sowing | A big benefit is possible in arid land |
| **Brazil landfill 2026** | Degraded site | **60% clay** was best for germination, survival and growth | Conflicts with Montana, so test it yourselves |
| **New Zealand 2025** | Temperate | Encapsulation **reduced** germination for many species and damaged large soft seeds, but about 20% of balls still gave seedlings | Pods can harm some seeds. Include bare seed as a comparison |
| **Otago: physical properties of seed balls** | Lab | Bentonite with coir or humate. **A 1:2 clay:humate ball fell apart almost immediately in water. Water crystals and biochar made balls too fragile.** Commercial and vermiculite balls survived shaking best | **Too much biochar can weaken the pod.** Vermiculite (a natural mineral) may add strength |
| **Ukrainian bentonite-humate study** | Lab | Strength depends on mix and moisture. They suggest an **outer layer of pure bentonite** for strength | A pure clay skin could help it survive the drop |
| ***Vachellia nilotica* seed balls** | Arid | **2 cm** thick worked best. 2.5 cm with more water did worse | Thickness can hurt. Keep the seed close to the surface |
| **Habitat Authority 2011** | California | Balls gave germination where broadcast seed gave none, but took a lot of labour | Making them is the cost problem |
| **Review of pelleting for arid land** | Global | Pellets don't greatly reduce germination and may help. Benefits for mechanical spreading aren't well measured | |
| **Brazil semi-arid, UFC** (methods only) | Caatinga | Seed balls of soil, compost, **cassava starch 5 to 15%**, and 0 to 1% hydrogel | Gives a useful recipe range for starch binder |

### 3.3 Seed coatings and treatments

- **Ren et al. 2026, *Earth's Future*** (meta-analysis): coatings raise germination **+23%** and biomass **+43%** across degraded ecosystems. Micronutrients and oxygen-supplying agents helped germination most. Microbial inoculants helped growth most. Under drought, the benefits held regardless of stress level. ([Wiley](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025EF007186))
- **Berto, Ritchie & Erickson 2024** (meta-analysis, native grasses): seed treatments improved germination 16% and emergence 22%, but **lab and glasshouse gains aren't translating to the field**. ([UWA](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/the-effects-of-seed-enhancements-on-plant-establishment-in-native/))
- **Pedrini et al. 2021, *PLOS ONE*:** seeds coated with **salicylic acid** (the active ingredient in aspirin, 0.1 mM) didn't germinate more, but **seedlings survived and grew better in the field**. ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8189473/), [Curtin](https://www.curtin.edu.au/research/news/curtin-study-finds-aspirin-takes-the-headache-out-of-restoration-2?type=media))
  - **(our analysis)** It's cheap, it's used in tiny amounts, and it targets survival, which is the weak point of pods. Worth testing.
- **Madsen et al. 2018, *PLOS ONE*:** primed seeds in extruded pods came up **5 to 15 days sooner** and gave up to 3.8 times more seedlings, though results depended on the soil. ([PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0204380))
  - **(our analysis)** Speed matters in the desert: come up before the soil dries.
- **Activated carbon pellets** (Swartz et al. 2026) increased emergence after herbicide treatment. ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/rec.70316), [PDF](https://projects.sare.org/media/pdf/S/w/a/Swartz_et_al_2026_Restoration-Ecology-2026-Swartz-Seed-pellets-containing-activated-carbon-increase-emergence-of-native-plant-species.pdf))
- **Fungicide seed coating** improved recruitment in dryland. ([Sowards 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/rec.14312))
- **Seed coatings that speed up germination** helped emergence, but seedlings grew less if water came later, which is a trade-off. ([Shriver, USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/seed-coating-treatments-alter-emergence-windows-native-intermountain-west-us-grasses))
- **Rodent-repellent coatings** (USDA): ghost pepper, neem oil and activated carbon cut eating by 47 to 50%. **Even a blank coating** gave 99% avoidance when other food was available. ([USDA FS](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/61381))
- **Natural hydrogels for seeds:** pure hydrogels struggle in dry soil, while cross-linked starch and CMC worked better. ([Nature Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-30594-1)) A 2024 study used **psyllium husk** (isabgol) as a natural hydrogel and noted that research on hydrogels for drone use is limited.

### 3.4 Burial and microsite studies (why where the seed sits matters)

- ***Calligonum*:** buried seeds gave **14.6%** emergence vs **2.7%** on the surface. Best depth was about 2 cm. ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5838076/), [bet-hedging study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6092557/))
- ***Haloxylon ammodendron*:** a light rain makes buried seeds germinate but **isn't enough for the seedlings to survive**. ([Australian J Botany](https://connectsci.au/bt/article-abstract/53/6/529/133061/Effects-of-irrigation-on-seedling-emergence-and?redirectedFrom=fulltext))
- **Sandstorms** permanently damage *Haloxylon* seedlings' photosynthesis. ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11738-023-03528-5))
- **(our analysis)** These four results give the design brief: get the seed slightly buried, keep it wet longer than one small rain would, and shelter the young seedling.

### 3.6 Advanced and concept carriers

- **CMU self-burying carrier** (Luo, Yao et al., *Nature* 2023): white oak veneer with three tails, inspired by *Erodium* seeds. **80% drilled in**, compared with as low as 0% for natural self-burying seeds on some ground. It **struggled in stormy conditions**, and making it takes 5 steps with chemical cleaning. I found no 2024 to 2025 follow-ups. A drone pilot is reportedly planned for the **Aral Sea**. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05656-3), [CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2023/february/biodegradable-seed-carrier.html), [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/self-drilling-seeders-break-new-ground/), [Aral Sea](https://inbusiness.kz/ru/last/aralskoe-more-hotyat-zasadit-s-pomoshyu-dronov))
- **Dew-collecting seed bomb concept** (Robohub 2016): shuttlecock-like vanes that collect night dew and channel it to the seed ball, opening on impact as anchors. A design idea only, never tested. ([Robohub](https://robohub.org/pushing-back-deserts-through-aerial-seeding))
- **Design concepts:** Jin-wook Hwang's "Seedbomb" capsules for desertification ([Inhabitat](https://inhabitat.com/seedbomb-plant-capsules-combat-desertification/close), [Yanko](https://www.yankodesign.com/2009/05/08/bomberman-explodes-plants/amp/)).
- **Maple-seed (samara) shapes, 3D-printed:** wind tests. Another 2025 paper tested 3D-printed seed pod shapes for drop, bounce and drone use. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12553), [Journal of Forests](https://archive.conscientiabeam.com/index.php/101/article/view/4397))
- **Teen inventor:** a seed pod made from food waste, with self-reported early germination up to 94%. Not peer-reviewed. ([Daily Gazette](https://www.dailygazette.com/tribune/seed-of-a-good-idea-student-earns-national-recognition-tackling-reforestation-climate-solutions/article_1164b624-5815-5ea3-9fe0-7fc86416cef9.html))

### 3.7 UAE and Gulf researchers

**Prof. Ali El-Keblawy's group (University of Sharjah, with UAEU co-authors)** is the closest academic match:
- Jarrar, El-Keblawy et al. 2023, "Seed enhancement technologies for sustainable dryland restoration: **coating and scarification**", *Science of the Total Environment* 904:166150. It recommends **combining coating and scarification** to beat different barriers. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37595910/), [UAEU](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/seed-enhancement-technologies-for-sustainable-dryland-restoration/))
- Jarrar, El-Keblawy et al. 2024, "**Seed priming** as a promising technique for sustainable restoration of dryland", *Restoration Ecology* (in the SER and KISR Arid Lands series). Limits: cost, how long seeds keep, and environmental concerns. ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/rec.14182), [UAEU](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/seed-priming-as-a-promising-technique-for-sustainable-restoration/))
- Jarrar & El-Keblawy 2022: coatings with water-holding materials raise a seed's water-holding capacity and help it make the most of rain. ([MDPI proceedings](https://doi.org/10.3390/environsciproc2022016001))
- They frame seed treatments as **coating = "add", scarification = "remove", priming = "expose"**, which is a tidy way to structure your design slide.
- The same group (El-Keblawy & Al-Shamsi 2008) studied *Haloxylon salicornicum* germination, and the group's papers cover many UAE halophytes. ([review](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-030-17854-3_45-1))
- **(our analysis)** If you're both students in the UAE, emailing this group to ask for advice, seeds, or a letter of support could greatly strengthen the "team competence" score. It's worth one polite email this week.

**Other UAE species research:** *Cyperus conglomeratus* germination ([UAEU thesis](https://scholarworks.uaeu.ac.ae/all_theses/651/)), *Calligonum* fruit colour ([Zayed University](https://zuscholars.zu.ac.ae/works/1279)), UAE native plants' drought tolerance ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12522444/)), Arabian AMF fungi ([UAEU/Basel](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8933382/)).

## 4. Lessons for our design

| # | Lesson | Evidence | Design decision (our analysis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pods help emergence, not long-term survival | Johnstone 2026, Stock 2020, NZ 2025 | Optimise for emergence in 8 to 10 weeks. Add one survival booster (salicylic acid?) |
| 2 | Lab gains often vanish in the field | Berto 2024 | Test outdoors in real sand and sun |
| 3 | Less clay can beat more clay | Teichroew 2025 (10% > 55%), vs Brazil (60% best) | Test 2 to 3 clay levels |
| 4 | Too much biochar or water crystals = fragile | Otago | Keep biochar low, or only in the core |
| 5 | A pure clay skin adds strength | Ukrainian study | Outer bentonite layer for the drop |
| 6 | Pods can set hard and trap seedlings | Stock 2020 (100% topsoil) | Include sand. Check seedlings can break out |
| 7 | Thicker isn't better | *Vachellia* 2 cm > 2.5 cm | Seeds near the surface, not deep |
| 8 | Buried beats surface | *Calligonum* 14.6% vs 2.7% | A pod that slumps into a mound when wet |
| 9 | A rain-cued coating has worked at scale | Alxa pelleting, Su 2017 starch SAP | Hard when dry, cracks or softens when wet |
| 10 | Dropping from the air can do worse than by hand | Thar 1999 | Flat shape that doesn't roll; drop-test it |
| 11 | Even a blank coating deters rodents | USDA | A cheap shell may be enough |
| 12 | Primed seed comes up days sooner | Madsen 2018 | Prime the medium-size seeds |
| 13 | Scarify plus coat together | El-Keblawy 2023 | Scarify *Calligonum*, then coat |
| 14 | Microbes help growth more than germination | Ren 2026, Johnstone 2026 | Optional, low priority for an 8 to 10 week test |
| 15 | One small rain isn't enough | *Haloxylon* irrigation study | The water-holding core is the key feature |

A second sweep covering drone seeding results, Gulf programmes, Chinese desert aerial seeding, new
carriers and patents, pellet trials, hydrogels, predation and strength testing. **Evidence labels:**
**[peer-reviewed]**, **[preprint]**, **[thesis]**, **[press]** (company or government announcement,
not independently checked).

### 9.1 The short version

1. **Still nobody has published field results for drone-dropped pods in the Arabian desert.**
   EAD, Qatar and Saudi Arabia have all seeded by drone; none has published how many seedlings
   came up.
2. **EAD's 2025 programme seeded our exact species on sand dunes.** It included *Calligonum*,
   *Haloxylon* and *Panicum* on the dunes of Qasr Al Sarab, with Dendra. That's the strongest hint
   yet that Nabat's validation sites will be dune sand.
3. **Johnstone 2026 is confirmed** (Crossref): seed balls helped seeds **emerge** but not plants
   **establish**, with no difference by shape, clay, nutrients or inoculant. A 2025 study reportedly found
   round vs. disc effects were species-specific (unverified, see §11.11). **Shape won't win germination; it has to win on
   drop, roll, cost and handling.**
4. **Less clay can be better in the field.** Teichroew & Rew 2025: 10% clay pellets gave 2.6
   seedlings against 1.2 for 55% clay. Their 2.5 cm pellets were more likely to produce seedlings,
   which supports our size. A Brazilian study found 60% clay best, so the right amount depends on
   the site. Test it.
5. **In Chinese desert trials, an outer film coating beat pelleting alone** (pelleting +22% seedling rate for
   *Caragana* in the Mu Us; film-coated "much better", no figure in the abstract). The film was Eudragit, a
   synthetic polymer, over an ethylcellulose and superabsorbent pellet, so it does not transfer directly to a
   no-microplastics pod. Su et al. 2015, *J. Beijing Forestry Univ.* 37(10): 96–102.
6. **Drop height affects germination** (EJFE 2026), so test seed viability after a 15 m drop, not
   just whether the pod stays whole.

## 10. Around the world: drone seeding, seed balls and pods outside the UAE (3 Nov 2026)

Same evidence labels as §9: **[peer-reviewed]**, **[preprint]**, **[thesis]**, **[report]**
(company or government report with data), **[press]** (announcement or claim, not independently checked).

### 10.1 The short version

1. **Almost nobody publishes outcomes.** Worldwide, programmes report *seeds dropped* or *hectares
   covered*, rarely *plants established*. The 2021 review of drone seeding (Mohan et al.) and the
   2019 review of seed pellets (Gornish et al.: 8 positive, 10 neutral and 9 negative effects
   across 24 publications) both say the evidence is thin and mixed.
2. **Where numbers do exist, direct seeding establishes poorly**, usually **5 to 25%**:

   | Where | Result |
   |---|---|
   | Patagonia | 4 to 22% survival from direct seeding, vs. 84 to 91% for planted seedlings (but 64% cheaper) |
   | Aral Sea | saxaul (*Haloxylon*) typically 20 to 30% |
   | Kenya | seed balls: 5 to 10% gave a shoot |
   | Haryana, India | about 15% germination |
   | Turkey | cedar drone sowing: 23.7%, falling to 12.8% within months |

   A pod that reliably beats about 20% in desert conditions would stand out.
3. **Where the seed ends up matters more than what it's wrapped in.** This shows up again and again:
   - Flash Forest found **embedded pods beat pods simply dropped on the surface**.
   - In Western Australia, a furrowing seeder beat pellets.
   - In the Karoo, tilling plus brush packing raised establishment.
   - Saudi Arabia aims seed at low spots where rainwater collects.
   - Camera-guided drones in Mexico and Spain choose the spot before dropping.
4. **A coating can lose its benefit while it sits in storage.** In Patagonia (2025), coating helped
   only when seeds were sown right after coating, and hydrogel often *reduced* emergence. Our pods
   will be made in December and dropped in January to February, so **test pods after storage, not
   just fresh ones**.
5. **Dry pods beat wet pods** (Flash Forest, Alberta report), and they're easier to transport.
6. **The closest overseas project to ours: Aral Sea, Kazakhstan (Oct 2025).** It uses
   Berkeley's self-burying E-SEED carriers dropped by drone to sow **saxaul (*Haloxylon*)**, the same
   genus as one of our species, on the dried seabed. Budget US$600,000. A 50 ha second phase in 2027
   goes ahead **only if survival beats 20%**. It's a ready-made benchmark for the deck.

## 11. Seed carrier design research in general (3 Nov 2026)

Studies of seed balls, pellets, coatings and capsules of any kind, not only drone-dropped ones,
organised by the design question each answers. Same evidence labels as §9 and §10.

### 11.1 The short version

1. **Where the seed sits inside the carrier is the single biggest design lever found.** Moving
   small seeds from the centre to the **edge** of a pellet raised emergence **28-fold on average**
   across 10 Australian species (Lieurance et al. 2024). Seeds at the **bottom** of a pellet sown on
   the surface did best for *Jacksonia* (Brown et al. 2023). **Our layered design is on the right track.**
2. **Carriers can do worse than bare seed.**
   - Mojave Desert shrubs: seed balls 0 to 5% emergence vs. **17 to 22%** for sown seed.
   - New Zealand: putting seed into balls generally reduced sprouting.
   - Conservation Evidence rates seed balls **"unknown effectiveness"**.
   - Pedrini 2017 titled their review *"Seed coating: science or marketing spin?"*

   So the bare-seed control in our tray test isn't a formality. It's the test.
3. **The best clay amount depends on seed size.** A Portuguese study found 25% clay best for small
   and medium seeds and 50 to 75% for large seeds. Montana: 10% beat 55%. Brazil: 60% best. Use
   **a different recipe for each layer**: low clay where the small seeds sit, more clay in the
   structural core.
4. **Biochar and water crystals can make a carrier fragile.** In the Otago study, balls with biochar
   or water crystals were too fragile to use, and in rice, more biochar meant a weaker coating.
   Keep biochar out of the structural body, or use only a little.
5. **Clusters of seeds push through soil crust.** Grouping seeds in one pellet raised emergence
   1.3 to 1.9 times in crust-forming clay (Madsen et al. 2012), but made no difference in sand.
   Useful if any Nabat sites have a surface crust.
6. **Shape still doesn't matter for emergence.** That's Johnstone 2026 (§9.4). No new study
   contradicts it.

### 11.10 What this means for our pod

| Evidence | Design change |
|---|---|
| Small seeds near the edge: **28 times better** emergence; seeds at the **bottom** face did best | **Press seeds into both flat faces**, not the core. Our ring is **symmetric top to bottom**, so whichever way up it lands, one seeded face touches the soil. A clear argument for the deck |
| Seeds in the centre: bigger seeds cope better | If anything goes in the core, make it the largest seed (*Calligonum*), as in the current layered plan |
| Best clay depends on seed size (25% small, 50 to 75% large) | Use **different recipes for different layers**: a low-clay seed skin of about 10 to 25%, and a structural core with more clay plus date-palm fibre for strength |
| Biochar and water crystals make balls fragile; more biochar means a weaker coating | Keep biochar to a few percent, and in the seed skin rather than the structural core. Crush-test with and without it |
| Gypsum binder: crumbles less and dissolves fast | Try **calcium sulphate (gypsum)** as a binder. It's cheap and found locally in the UAE's salt flats and desert. Test it for salt load: *Cyperus* is salt-sensitive |
| PVAc binder harmful; starch fine | Use starch or CMC binders only |
| Seed clusters break crust (clay only) | Group the tiny *Eremobium* and *Cyperus* seeds in small clusters on each face. It costs nothing and helps if the sites have crust |
| Seed balls can do worse than bare seed | Keep the bare-seed control in every tray test, and report it honestly |
| Low-dose microbes do nothing | Leave microbes out, or use high-dose local soil as an optional extra arm |
| Pressed earthen pellets were used at scale in the 1950s | Historical precedent for pressing as a production method (production slide) |

## 13. Where the seeds should sit inside the pod: the evidence (5 Nov 2026)

Answers "capsule, mixed through, or a surface layer?" plus how deep each of our six species should be.
Same evidence labels as earlier sections.

### 13.1 The short version

1. **Seeds near the surface beat seeds in the centre, by a lot.** Small seeds near the edge of a pellet came
   up **28 times better** than seeds in the centre; for half the species, as well as bare seed (Lieurance
   et al. 2024). With seeds in the centre, **bare seed generally beat the pellets**.
2. **Seeds at the bottom of a pellet did best** (Brown et al. 2023: 70% vs. 57% for bare seed sown 12 mm deep;
   seeds in the middle of the pellet, 32%).
3. **Enclosing seeds can hurt.** An Ontario report found encapsulating black spruce seed had "an
   unacceptably adverse effect on germination", while ordinary pelleting didn't. In shallot and banana
   synthetic-seed work, uncoated or single-layer versions did better than encapsulated or hollow ones.
   **A hollow capsule is the weakest option.**
4. **Thicker coats lower emergence** (Brazil, native trees), and a 2 cm seed ball beat 2.5 cm (*Vachellia*).
5. **Industry has already patented a "seeds on the outside" design.** Seeds sit in a thin adhesive layer
   **around** an inert core, which is our "seed skin" idea. One version uses a water-soluble core so the
   pill collapses and drops the seeds onto the soil (US 5,623,781 for seeds; US 5,389,115 applies the same method to spores).
6. **No study directly compares "mixed through" with "surface layer"** inside a seed ball. Our tray test
   (ring mixed through vs. ring with a seed skin) would be new data.

### 13.4 What this means for our design

| Option | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hollow capsule, seeds inside | **No** | Seeds in the centre do far worse; encapsulation hurt germination in several studies; hollow is weaker in a drop |
| Seeds mixed through the whole pod | **Acceptable in our thin ring, poor in a ball** | 93% of a ring is within 3 mm of the surface (about 50% for a ball); no species gets its own depth |
| **Seed skin on both faces + a *Calligonum* pocket** | **Recommended** | Matches the edge (28×) and bottom-of-pellet results, the "seeds around a core" patent idea, and each species' best depth |
| Seeds stuck on the surface under sand dust | Good for *Cyperus* and *Panicum* | Both emerge best at the surface; sand dust deters ants |

**Tray tests to add:** *Eremobium* in light vs. dark (surface vs. 2 mm cover). The four-way comparison
(bare seed, mixed ball, mixed ring, seed-skin ring) would be new data: no published study compares
"mixed through" with "surface layer" directly.
